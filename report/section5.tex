\section{Explainability}

We looked at the trained CNN with three methods on validation images
(Fig.~\ref{fig:explanation}): vanilla-gradient saliency, Grad-CAM on the last
convolutional block, and occlusion sensitivity (we slide a neutral grey patch
over the image and check how much $P(\mathrm{AI})$ changes). We picked the most
borderline cases for each category: true-positive (AI), true-negative (real),
false-positive, and false-negative.


\textbf{What we found}\\
Saliency turned out pretty noisy and spread out over the whole image instead of
pointing at one object. Grad-CAM often highlighted background or sky instead of
the actual subject, which fits with a model that's picking up on general
texture statistics rather than what's actually in the picture. We trust the 
occlusion result more than saliency or Grad-CAM here, since it directly measures 
the effect on the output rather than relying on gradients. The occlusion
analysis was the most useful of the three: when we covered up a patch with grey,
$P(\mathrm{AI})$ almost always went up, meaning the model treats flat,
low-detail regions as ``AI-like''. Averaged over many images
(Fig.~\ref{fig:occlusion}), this effect is strongest in the center of the image.

Interestingly, the occlusion effect is noticeably stronger on real images than 
on AI ones. This suggests the model's decision 
for real images depends more heavily on local high-detail regions, whereas for AI 
images the 'fake' signal is spread more evenly across the whole image, consistent 
with AI images being globally smoother.

This highlights two things the model is doing wrong: it confuses smoothness with
``fake'' (because AI images tend to be smoother), and it has a slight bias
toward the center of the image. It also explains our errors: false positives are
smooth, low-detail real photos, and false negatives are unusually detailed AI
images. So overall, we shouldn't fully trust these explanations. The model is
relying on a real but shortcut-prone signal (local detail/smoothness), not
something robust.
